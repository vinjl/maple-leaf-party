## 0. Stepped care (upstream of the covenant)

See **`11-food-health-nation.md`**: lifestyle and foundations **first**, pharmaceuticals **last**; extreme restriction of psychoactive meds for minors (depression/ADHD especially), with emergency exceptions. Reduces load on the catastrophic pool.

## 1. Problem

Canada runs a politically sacred healthcare brand that in practice delivers:

- **Queues** for serious care  
- **Unlimited minor/routine demand** at zero price  
- **Open-ended cost growth** that locks high taxes  
- Eligibility and usage patterns that are not strictly **citizen-first**  
- Weak incentives against preventable self-harm that socializes costs onto workers and capital

### Preventable disease is a huge share of the bill

All-government health spending sits on a **~$300–400B** scale in recent Canadian public data **[VERIFY CIHI year]**.  
A large fraction of illness and cost is **preventable** (diet quality, alcohol, smoking/nicotine, inactivity, metabolic neglect).  

Directional planning band used in Maple projections (`19-national-projections.md`):

| Band | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **15–25%+** of system load | Attributable to preventable lifestyle disease (order-of-magnitude, not a finished actuarial table) |
| **Tens of billions $/year** | Fiscal room if that load falls (plus second-order disability and productivity gains) |

**Moral failure of the status quo:** people who keep effort (train, eat real food, stay off the heavy bottle and smoke) are taxed to fund the optional wreckage of those who will not try. That punishes health and rewards freeloading. It is unfair for the neighbour who exercises daily and barely uses the system to pay the preventable bill of people who refuse basic responsibility after the brain is fully developed.

### Age 25 is the responsibility line

| Band | Rule | Why |
|------|------|-----|
| **Under 25** | Public cover **free regardless of lifestyle** | Brain still developing; not yet fully adult agency over long-horizon habits |
| **25+** | Free catastrophic pool only with **verified health effort** (medical exceptions) | Adult accountability for preventable disease risk |
| **Always** | Emergency stabilization | No dying in the street for a covenant fail |

### Private care as competence engine

Shrinking open-ended public freeloading does not abandon care. It **opens room for private delivery** on non-emergency and non-covenant demand:

- Private providers **can fail** and cannot rely on automatic taxpayer bailouts, so they must be competent on quality, speed, and price.  
- Public monopolies are too often incentivized by **funding and political rescue**, not by patient results.  
- Tax cuts from a lighter preventable load let citizens **buy insurance, private care, or pay when needed**.  
- The public still **pays the serious bill** when the adult has shown good health habits (covenant pass) or is under 25.

Arithmetic: you cannot be **Switzerland on taxes** and **all-you-can-eat free medicine for everyone and everything** forever.

## 2. Design goals

1. **Citizens first** — public system is for Canadians, not the world.  
2. **Insure bad luck, not unlimited lifestyle freeloading.**  
3. **Reward health effort** — covenant pass = free catastrophic pool; refuse preventable harm = self-pay non-emergency (insurance / private / cash).  
4. **Serious care is protected** — cancer, trauma, heart attack, necessary surgery get capacity, not wait behind trivial demand.  
5. **Minor care is not free** — price signals + HSAs + private options.  
6. **Health Covenant** — free catastrophic coverage is earned by baseline health maintenance (with medical exceptions).  
7. **Emergency stabilization** always for citizens under the system — no dying in the street for a lifestyle fail.  
8. **Fund tax cuts** — lighter preventable load returns room to citizens so private cover and out-of-pocket care are realistic.

## 3. Public promise (plain language)

### What is free (public) for covenant-eligible **citizens**

- True **emergencies** and major trauma  
- **Cancer** diagnosis and treatment pathways  
- Heart attacks, strokes, major cardiovascular events  
- Medically necessary major surgery  
- Serious chronic disease treatment (with adherence expectations where clinically appropriate)  
- Other clearly **catastrophic / major** conditions on a published schedule

### What is **not** free

- Minor and routine care  
- Convenience / trivial clinic use  
- Non-serious issues  
- Purely lifestyle-optional services  
- Cosmetic and low-value interventions without strong evidence

**Payment for non-free care:** out-of-pocket, **Health Savings Accounts** (tax-advantaged; linked to IDS where sensible), or private insurance.

## 4. Eligibility gates

### Gate A — Citizenship

| Status | Public catastrophic basket | Notes |
|--------|----------------------------|-------|
| Canadian citizen (covenant pass) | Yes | Core promise |
| Canadian citizen (covenant fail) | Emergency stabilization; limited/paid pathway for non-emergency major care | See §6 |
| Permanent resident | Transition rules only; not the end-state equal of citizens | Tight, time-limited [policy choice to lock] |
| Temporary visitor / worker | Emergency stabilization + cost recovery / insurance | Not a second free system |
| Illegal entrant | Emergency stabilization + removal pathway; cost recovery | No reward queue |

**End-state Maple position:** public health insurance is a **citizen institution**. PR transition is a temporary political-legal bridge, not a philosophy.

### Gate B — Health Covenant (for free catastrophic coverage)

Must be **provable**, **periodic**, and **exception-capable**.

## 5. Health Covenant standards

Exact clinical thresholds to be set with physicians in implementation. Directional rules:

| Domain | Pass condition | Path if failing |
|--------|----------------|-----------------|
| **Nicotine** | Non-smoker / non-user of recreational nicotine products | Verified cessation program; time-limited conditional pass while compliant |
| **Alcohol** | Below abuse thresholds (screening tools + biomarkers as appropriate) | Treatment engagement pathway |
| **Illicit hard drugs** | Clean or treatment-engaged with verification | Treatment-first + legal leverage where appropriate |
| **Metabolic health** | Prefer **markers** (e.g. waist, A1c, blood pressure bands) over crude BMI alone; within band **or** supervised improvement with documented progress | Supervised program; medical exceptions |
| **Preventive basics** | Age-appropriate evidence-based screens for continued *non-emergency* eligibility | Complete screens |

### Medical exceptions (mandatory fairness)

Documented conditions that make standards inappropriate (certain endocrine disorders, disabilities, medications, etc.) receive **clinical exception status** — covenant is not a club for bullying the unlucky.

### Addiction as disease vs freeloading

- Addiction: **treatment pathway** with verification beats pure exile.  
- Refusal of all engagement + repeated preventable cost imposition: **financial and priority consequences** for non-emergency care.  
- Never conflate “has cancer” with “refuses every standard of self-care.”

## 6. Pass / fail consequences

### Covenant PASS

- Full public catastrophic / serious-care basket  
- Priority design that protects serious care capacity  
- Minimal friction for true major illness (this is the social insurance)

### Covenant FAIL

| Care type | Treatment |
|-----------|-----------|
| Emergency stabilization | **Always** for citizens |
| Minor / routine | Already not free (same as pass) |
| Non-emergency serious care | Pay risk-rated contribution **or** regain eligibility via compliance path before elective scheduling |
| Public messaging | Failure is about **contribution conditions**, not human worth |

### Compliance restoration

Published, time-bound path: enroll → verified progress → provisional pass → full pass. No Kafka maze.

---

## 7. Why minor care exits the free basket

Economics of zero-price demand:

- Overuse of scarce clinician time  
- Queues that delay **cancer and surgery**  
- Political inability to control cost → permanent high tax  
- Suppresses private capacity and innovation at the low-acuity layer  

**Logic:** free price belongs on **low-frequency, high-severity** events (insurance). High-frequency, low-severity events need **prices, HSAs, and competition**.

---

## 8. Health Savings Accounts (HSA)

- Tax-advantaged accounts for minor/routine care and cost-sharing  
- Can integrate with IDS (family medical saving as allowed contribution class — optional design)  
- Portable; citizen-owned  
- Employer contributions allowed under simple rules  

---

## 9. Private delivery & capacity

- Allow private delivery options for both minor care and (under regulated terms) faster serious care **without** destroying the citizen catastrophic floor  
- Goal is **capacity expansion**, not two-tier cruelty theatre  
- Foreign-trained credential recognition accelerated where safety is met  

Requires careful **Canada Health Act reform** — see §11.

---

## 10. Spending shift inside the public basket

Even within catastrophic care, redirect from:

- Admin bloat, low-value procedures, political headcount  

Toward:

- Surgical throughput, diagnostics, cancer pathways, emergency capacity, mental health modalities with outcome evidence, prevention ROI that reduces catastrophe rates  

**Metric:** median wait for designated serious procedures ↓ year over year.

## 11. Federal vs provincial map

| Lever | Actor |
|-------|-------|
| Canada Health Act rewrite / conditions | Federal |
| Transfer money tied to Covenant + catastrophic basket + wait metrics | Federal |
| Delivery (hospitals, clinicians) | Provincial |
| Covenant credential data standards | Federal standard, provincial operation |
| HSA tax treatment | Federal (+ provincial tax coordination) |
| Immigration health rules | Federal |

### Provincial Compact (Maple-ready)

Offer provinces:

1. Higher flexibility on private delivery and minor-care pricing  
2. Stable transfers for catastrophic citizen care  
3. Shared digital Covenant credential  
4. Capital funding for surgical/diagnostic capacity  

In exchange:

1. Publish waits and outcomes  
2. Implement citizenship + covenant eligibility gates  
3. Remove pure free minor-care from public basket on an agreed schedule  

Provinces that refuse can keep old model **without** full access to new transfer growth streams.

## 12. Charter and legal framing [VERIFY]

This section is policy design, **not** a legal opinion.

**Intended frame:**

- Public insurance **eligibility** and **benefit design**  
- Contribution / behavioural conditions with **accommodation** for disability and medical exception  
- Resource allocation to protect serious care  
- Citizenship as membership institution for social insurance  

**Risk zones:** ss. 7 and 15 Charter challenges; CHA litigation; disability discrimination claims.

**Mitigations:**

- Emergency care retained  
- Medical exceptions  
- Least-impairing compliance paths  
- Evidence record on cost, waits, and behavioural elasticity  
- Legislative precision over campaign crudeness

## 13. Transition path (avoid system shock)

| Stage | Content |
|-------|---------|
| T0 | Publish basket schedule + covenant draft standards; open consult with clinicians |
| T1 | HSAs live; minor care co-pays begin phased |
| T2 | Covenant registration voluntary → then mandatory for *new* non-emergency major coverage |
| T3 | Full catastrophic-only public basket; citizenship gate enforced for new enrolment |
| T4 | Transfer and tax cut phases unlock as savings materialize |

Grandfathering rules for people mid-treatment: **do not yank active cancer protocols**.

## 15. Messaging discipline

**Say:**  
“Public healthcare is insurance for when life hits hard — for citizens who hold up their end. Minor care isn’t free. That is how we cut taxes and clear OR time for cancer.”

**Don’t say:**  
“Get sick and die if you’re overweight.” (Inaccurate and self-sabotaging. Emergency exists; exceptions exist; restoration exists; unlucky disease is covered when covenant held.)
