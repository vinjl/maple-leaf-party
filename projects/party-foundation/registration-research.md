## 2. High-level path (federal)

Directional summary from public Elections Canada materials:

1. **Organize** as a party with the primary purpose of participating in public affairs by endorsing candidates.  
2. **Appoint** required officers (e.g. leader, party officers as required), **financial agent**, and **auditor** meeting statutory criteria.  
3. **File application** for registration with the Chief Electoral Officer with required statements, names, addresses, and documentation.  
4. **Name rules:** proposed name (and short-form) must not be confusing with existing parties / prohibited names — **“The Maple Leaf” / “Maple Leaf Party of Canada” must be clearance-checked**.  
5. **Eligible vs registered:** parties may become *eligible* upon meeting application requirements; full *registered* status historically ties to running a candidate in a general election or by-election (confirm current Act thresholds — law evolved after older 50-candidate rules).  
6. **Ongoing:** strict financial reporting, contribution rules, expense limits, privacy of certain electors lists uses, etc.

**[VERIFY]** all numeric thresholds, forms, and definitions against the live Elections Canada checklist before any application.

---

## 3. Benefits of registration (why bother)

Public materials note registered parties may gain, among other things:

- Party affiliation on ballots for endorsed candidates  
- Access to certain broadcasting time allocations in general elections  
- Donation tax credit treatment for contributors (subject to rules)  
- Expense reimbursement formulas when legal thresholds met  

Exact benefits change with legislation — confirm at time of registration.

## 5. Name risk

“Maple Leaf” is culturally generic and symbolically loaded (including military/heritage uses). Risks:

- Confusion with existing or historical political entities  
- Trademark conflicts in commercial use (logos, merch, domain)  
- Elections Canada similarity rejection  

**Action before launch branding spend:** formal name search + backup names list (e.g. “Maple Leaf Party of Canada”, “The Maple Leaf Party”, distinctive bilingual forms).

## 6. Sequencing recommendation

1. Finish and stabilize platform texts (current phase).  
2. Name clearance.  
3. Founding constitution + membership rules.  
4. Officers, financial agent, auditor.  
5. Application for eligibility.  
6. Public launch + first candidate opportunity when ready — not before books/compliance exist.

**Do not** fundraise under casual structures that violate contribution rules. Get compliance right first.

---

## 7. Provincial parties

Federal and provincial parties are **separate** legal creatures in Canada. A federal Maple Leaf does not automatically create Ontario/Alberta/etc. parties. Decide later whether provincial wings are in scope.
